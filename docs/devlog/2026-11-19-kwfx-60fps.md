# 2026-11-19 — Keyword aura pictures at 60 a second (and why not faster)

Follow-up to `2026-11-18-perf-keyword-loops.md` (PR #2789, rule R-PERF-KEYWORD-AURA-01). The first cut baked Rise,
Execute and Rebirth's crown at 30 pictures a second and Flurry at 60. The owner plays at 240 Hz and asked whether the
auras could run faster than 60, and whether 30 really saves much. Standing rule (2026-11-19): a perf fix keeps
animations as smooth as authored unless the cost is massive.

**Shipped: all four auras at 60 pictures a second, baked by default.** `capture.mjs --fps N` re-bakes at another rate.

## How fast can Chromium play an animated image?

`scripts/kwfx/fps-probe.mjs`: 240-frame AVIF and WebP probes with the frame index painted in, one per rate, read back
from screenshots and from the compositor trace (`ImageAnimationController::WillActivate`), on a 240 Hz clock.

| rate in the file | played | refreshes of a 240 Hz display per picture |
|---|---|---|
| 30 | 30.1 | 7 to 9 (8 ideal) |
| 60 | 60.1 (WebP 58.9) | 3 to 5 (4 ideal) |
| 90 | 89.9 (WebP 90.7) | 1 to 4, uneven (2.67 ideal) |
| 100, 120, 240 | **9.7 to 10.1** | 23 to 25 |

**The ceiling is about 90.** Blink holds any frame of 10 ms or less for 100 ms (an old rule for broken GIFs), so 100 a
second and up play at 10. Between 60 and 90 a picture lands on an uneven number of refreshes on 60, 144 and 240 Hz
displays; 60 divides 60, 120 and 240 Hz evenly. `capture.mjs` refuses `--fps` above 90.

## Cost (prod bench build, headless Chrome on the RTX 4080, ANGLE D3D11, 240 Hz)

`scripts/kwfx/measure.mjs --threads`, idle shop, 7 board + 7 hand minions, two passes of each config interleaved
(other builders were running: the bare shop with no aura at all read 4.2 to 9.0 ms between passes, so read pass-to-pass
spread as noise). Frame ms and % over 6.25 ms are per pass. Decode is image decode + upload on Chrome's worker threads
(off the main thread); GPU MB is the GPU process working set.

**All four auras on 14 cards** (the worst board):

| | frame ms | % > 6.25 ms | GPU main ms/s | decode ms/s | decodes/s | GPU MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| old live DOM | 53.95 / 52.92 | 100 / 100 | 957 / 672 | 0 | 0 | 375 / 384 |
| baked 30 (first cut) | 4.20 / 4.41 | 0.8 / 0.6 | 759 / 587 | 278 / 176 | 93 | 326 / 334 |
| **baked 60 (shipped)** | 4.33 / 4.36 | 2.7 / 0.7 | 1227 / 715 | 362 / 262 | 308 / 204 | 430 / 407 |
| baked 90 | 4.34 / 4.37 | 0.9 / 2.2 | 714 / 730 | 417 / (outlier) | 360 / 66 | 467 / 394 |
| baked 60, Flurry kept live | 5.95 / 11.40 | 32 / 97 | 962 / 985 | 231 / 340 | 180 / 173 | 419 / 415 |

One Rise card: decode 10 / 16 ms/s at 30, 23 / 22 at 60, 76 / 39 at 90. Fourteen Rise cards: 4.24 / 4.17 ms at 30,
5.20 / 4.29 at 60 (the 5.20 pass also read 14.6% over budget against 0.3% in the other: noise), 5.63 / 4.18 at 90.
The raw rows (every scene, both passes) are in the PR; the JSON is in `.claude/perf-scope-2026-11-18/kwperf-2026-11-19/` on the dev machine (not in git).

Reading it: going from 30 to 60 changes no frame time we can separate from the noise. It costs about +90 to +180 ms/s
of decode spread over Chrome's worker threads, more GPU-process time, and about +75 to +100 MB in the GPU process
with every aura on screen. The main thread does nothing either way. 90 costs more again for an uneven cadence.

**Bytes** (desktop only: phones never fetch these, `KWFX_ENABLED`): 1.82 MB at 30 -> **2.99 MB at 60 (+1.17 MB)**,
fetched once after boot. 90 would add about another 35% (Rise 419 KB vs 304, Execute 417 vs 306).

## Higher than 90: the alternatives tried

- **Keep Flurry live** (the fast one, about 260 px a second at the blade tips), after removing its no-op
  `mix-blend-mode: screen` (white rings blending only with each other inside a stacking context: plain alpha) and its
  idle breathe layer. Still 7 layers and animations a card: 14 Flurry cards 4.8 to 7.5 ms with 11 to 71% of frames over
  budget; with all four keywords 6.0 / 11.4 ms. Rejected as the default. The two clean-ups stay: they are what phones and
  the not-yet-loaded fallback render.
- **Interpolate on a shared canvas.** A worker held every aura's frames once in atlases and drew each card's canvas on
  every display frame as a cross-fade of the two pictures around "now" (exact on premultiplied pixels). 14 canvases:
  0.13 ms of drawing a frame at 238 fps, but **GPU main 640 ms/s against 142** for the same 14 animated images, and the
  56-canvas run never finished loading its atlases. About 4x the GPU cost for a blend, not new motion. Rejected.
- Two stacked images cross-faded by an opacity animation, and a lower-res stepped atlas: not built. A cross-fade needs
  two animated images per card advancing half a frame apart, which an animated image cannot be offset to do (§3p), and
  an atlas was already ruled out on 2026-11-18 (an atlas-sized layer per card).

## Clips

`scripts/kwfx/clips.mjs`, in `.claude/perf-scope-2026-11-18/clips/aura-fps/` (not in git): per aura, `<aura>-240hz.mp4`
(one video frame per 240 Hz display frame) and `<aura>-quarter-speed.mp4`, columns live / 90 / 60 / 30, the card at 2x
device pixels. A real-time recording could not show this: Chrome's screencast and screenshots top out near 30 a
second, and an animated image does not advance under headless begin-frame control (tried). So each display frame is
made by seeking the live aura to that instant; a baked column holds the picture its rate would show. That is the best
case for 90 (the probe measured uneven 1 to 4 refresh holds where the clip shows 2 to 3).

## Other changes in this pass

- `kwfx.ts`: `localStorage['ascent.kwfx.live'] = 'flurry'` (a comma list) keeps named auras live, to compare by eye;
  default none. An aura kept live does not fetch its picture.
- `variants.mjs`: every aura at 60. Rebirth keeps its 13.75 s loop: a 7.2 s loop would have halved its bytes but
  changed an ember's speed by 9%.
- `measure.mjs --threads` (per-thread busy time, decode work, process memory; `threads.mjs`), new scenes `R1`, `W1`,
  `V1`, `RB1`, `four`.
